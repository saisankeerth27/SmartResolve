# Escalation Procedures

## Overview

This document defines when and how issues should be escalated to higher tiers of support or specialized teams.

## Escalation Tiers

### Tier 1: Frontline Support

Handles standard customer inquiries and simple troubleshooting. Can resolve most issues on first contact. Escalates to Tier 2 if issue is not resolved within 15 minutes or requires specialized knowledge. Maximum authority for credits up to $25.

### Tier 2: Technical Support

Handles complex technical issues and advanced troubleshooting. Can access network diagnostic tools and deeper account information. Escalates to Tier 3 if issue requires network-side investigation or vendor coordination. Maximum authority for credits up to $100.

### Tier 3: Engineering and Specialists

Handles network-level issues, vendor coordination, and complex billing disputes. Can make configuration changes on network equipment. Escalates to Tier 4 if issue requires vendor TAC support or architectural changes. Maximum authority for credits up to $500.

### Tier 4: Vendor Support

Engaged for hardware failures, complex software issues, and manufacturer defects. Provides TAC (Technical Assistance Center) support. Coordinates RMA (Return Merchandise Authorization) for defective equipment. Resolution timelines are governed by vendor SLAs.

## Escalation Triggers

### Time-Based Escalation

If a Tier 1 representative cannot resolve an issue within 15 minutes, the issue must be escalated. If a Tier 2 engineer cannot resolve an issue within 4 hours, the issue must be escalated. If a Tier 3 specialist cannot resolve an issue within 24 hours, the issue must be escalated. Time-based escalations ensure issues are resolved within SLA.

### Severity-Based Escalation

P1 (Critical) issues are automatically escalated to Tier 3 upon creation. P2 (High) issues are automatically escalated to Tier 2 upon creation. P3 (Medium) issues are handled by Tier 1 and escalated if needed. P4 (Low) issues are handled by Tier 1.

### Customer-Requested Escalation

Customers have the right to request escalation at any time. The representative must honor the escalation request. The representative must document the reason for the escalation. The customer must be provided with the new case owner's contact information.

## Escalation Process

### Step 1: Document the Issue

Before escalating, the current owner must document: a summary of the issue, all troubleshooting steps attempted, current status and next steps, any customer commitments made, and the reason for escalation.

### Step 2: Notify the Customer

The customer must be notified before the escalation takes place. The customer must be provided with the reason for the escalation. The customer must be provided with the new case owner's name and contact information. The customer must be provided with an updated resolution timeline.

### Step 3: Transfer Ownership

The case is transferred to the appropriate escalation tier. All documentation is transferred with the case. The new owner reviews the case within 30 minutes of transfer. The new owner contacts the customer within 2 hours to confirm ownership.

### Step 4: Follow Up

The original owner follows up with the escalation team within 24 hours. If the escalation is not progressing, the original owner escalates further. The customer is updated at least once per day until resolution.

## Escalation Contacts

### NOC Team

Network Operations Center handles network-related escalations. Contact via the escalation bridge channel. Available 24/7 for P1 and P2 issues. Available during business hours for P3 and P4 issues.

### Billing Investigation Team

Handles complex billing disputes and payment issues. Contact via the billing escalation queue. Available during business hours. Response within 4 hours for standard issues, 1 hour for critical issues.

### Customer Relations Team

Handles complaint resolution and retention-sensitive cases. Contact via the customer relations queue. Available during business hours. Response within 2 hours for standard issues.

### Legal and Compliance

Handles legal inquiries, regulatory complaints, and compliance issues. Contact via the legal escalation queue. Available during business hours. Response within 24 hours.

## Escalation Documentation

### Escalation Notes

All escalations must include detailed notes in the case management system. Notes should include the timeline of events, customer impact, root cause (if known), actions taken, and recommended next steps. This documentation is essential for the escalation team to quickly understand and resolve the issue.

### Post-Escalation Review

All P1 and P2 escalations require a post-escalation review. The review includes the escalation timeline, resolution effectiveness, and customer satisfaction. Lessons learned are documented and shared with the team. Process improvements are identified and implemented.
