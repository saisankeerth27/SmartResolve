# Complaint Handling Procedures

## Overview

This document describes how SmartConnect handles customer complaints, from initial receipt through resolution and follow-up.

## Complaint Categories

### Service Quality Complaints

Complaints about the quality of voice, data, or messaging service. Includes dropped calls, slow data, poor coverage, and service outages. These complaints require technical investigation and may involve network engineering.

### Billing Complaints

Complaints about charges, payments, or account billing. Includes incorrect charges, unauthorized changes, and billing disputes. These complaints require billing investigation and may involve financial adjustments.

### Customer Service Complaints

Complaints about the quality of customer service interactions. Includes rude representatives, long wait times, and unresolved issues. These complaints require quality review and may involve additional training.

### Product and Policy Complaints

Complaints about product features, pricing, or company policies. Includes plan limitations, contract terms, and feature availability. These complaints require policy review and may involve management decision.

## Complaint Handling Process

### Step 1: Receive and Acknowledge

All complaints are received through any customer contact channel. The complaint is acknowledged within 4 hours for standard complaints, 1 hour for urgent complaints. A case number is provided to the customer. The complaint is categorized and prioritized.

### Step 2: Investigate

The assigned representative investigates the complaint thoroughly. This includes reviewing account history, service records, and any relevant documentation. The representative may contact the customer for additional information. The investigation is documented in the case management system.

### Step 3: Resolve

The representative determines the appropriate resolution based on the investigation. Resolution may include service restoration, billing adjustment, policy exception, or other corrective action. The resolution is communicated to the customer clearly and completely. The customer is asked to confirm acceptance of the resolution.

### Step 4: Follow Up

The representative follows up with the customer within 7 days of resolution. The follow-up confirms the resolution is working and the customer is satisfied. If the customer is not satisfied, the case is escalated for further review. The follow-up is documented in the case management system.

## Complaint Prioritization

### Urgent Complaints

Complaints involving safety concerns, regulatory issues, or significant financial impact. Response within 1 hour. Resolution within 24 hours. Escalation to management required.

### High Priority Complaints

Complaints involving service outages affecting multiple customers, billing errors over $100, or repeated unresolved issues. Response within 4 hours. Resolution within 48 hours. Escalation to supervisor required.

### Standard Complaints

General complaints about service quality, billing, or customer service. Response within 24 hours. Resolution within 72 hours. Handled by frontline representatives.

## Complaint Resolution Standards

### Fair Resolution

All complaints must be resolved fairly and equitably. The resolution must consider the customer's perspective and the company's policies. The resolution must be documented with a clear rationale. The resolution must be consistent with similar complaints.

### Customer Communication

The customer must be kept informed throughout the complaint process. Updates are provided at least every 24 hours during investigation. The customer must be notified of any delays. All communication must be professional and empathetic.

### Documentation

All complaint details, investigation findings, and resolutions must be documented. Documentation must be accurate and complete. Documentation is used for quality review, trend analysis, and training. Documentation is retained for the required period.

## Complaint Escalation

### Customer-Initiated Escalation

Customers may request escalation at any point during the complaint process. The representative must honor the escalation request. The representative must document the reason for the escalation. The customer must be provided with the new case owner's contact information.

### Managerial Escalation

Complaints that cannot be resolved by the frontline representative are escalated to a supervisor. Managerial escalation includes all P1 complaints, complaints involving potential legal action, and complaints that have been unresolved for more than 72 hours. The supervisor must review and respond within 4 hours.

### Executive Escalation

Complaints that cannot be resolved at the managerial level are escalated to executive management. Executive escalation includes complaints from regulatory bodies, complaints involving significant financial exposure, and complaints that have been unresolved for more than 7 days. Executive management must review and respond within 24 hours.
